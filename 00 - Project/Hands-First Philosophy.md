# Hands-First

> [!note] Status
> Educational Services' explicit, department-wide teaching philosophy — set by
> Steve, defined by Franz. This is the authority `teaching-philosophy.md`'s own
> "doing-centered" principle already anticipated; that document is Emerson
> Workbench's own operationalization of what's stated here, not a competing or
> separate philosophy.
>
> This document is vision and rationale — **not terminology to tag content
> with.** The five terms below explain *why* hands-on learning works; they are
> never applied to a slide, a module, or a piece of structure as a label. If a
> future change proposes tagging content with one of these five words
> directly, that proposal is wrong on its face — bring it back to prose.

## The philosophy

Hands-first prioritizes learning through action. Students engage content
directly by:

- **Contact** — places learning in the student's hands.
- **Assembly** — physically builds and deconstructs learning content.
- **Mechanical Operation** — understands purpose through function.
- **Instrumentation** — leverages tools and controls actions.
- **Measurement** — defines quality and creates standards.

Hands-first ensures student time is focused on the actions they will be
performing.

> [!note] A naming note, not a rule
> "Instrumentation" above is a *mode of engagement* (using tools and controls
> to act), not the same thing as `teaching-philosophy.md`'s Axis-1 domain value
> `instrumentation` (a subject-matter category — positioners, digital valve
> controllers). The two only look like the same word. Because this document's
> own terms are never used as content tags (see the status note above), the
> two never actually collide in the schema — flagged here only so a future
> reader isn't confused by the coincidence.

## The rule for content structure

**No long lecture blocks.** Any time content shows or explains something, it
must be followed — once that explanation genuinely finishes, not partway
through it — by a doing activity. That activity must be a genuinely strong,
compelling activity in its own right, not a token check-the-box exercise.

**This is a judgment about topic completion, not a slot count, a module
boundary, or a competency change.** A topic is however much material actually
belongs together before the learner is ready to act on it — sometimes that's
one concept, sometimes it's many. Two real examples, opposite ends of the same
rule:

- **14101's Fisher 657 actuator topic** genuinely needs many slides run
  *uninterrupted* — identifying the actuator, understanding bench set, mounting
  it, servicing it are one continuous arc a learner needs whole before doing
  anything with it. An activity forced partway through would break a topic
  that isn't finished yet.
- **IfE's Foundations module** covers several distinct topics (the Five
  Tenets, Show-Tell-Do, reading a training solution, writing objectives) in
  one module. An activity forced after *every single one* — including the
  thin, framing-only ones — would be puerile and disruptive, the opposite of
  hands-first: it substitutes a mechanical rhythm for a real judgment about
  when a learner is actually ready to act.

The two examples above are not the same shape — that's the point. The
discipline is real, sourced, compelling activity design at the moments where
it genuinely serves the learner, not a repeating pattern applied on a
schedule. Confirmed directly, not assumed (Content-Depth investigation,
2026-09-10): no field in the current schema reliably marks where a topic
ends independent of module or competency boundaries — checked both, both
fail against these two real examples in opposite directions. Enforcement is
human review for now (`Course Porting Pipeline.md`, Working Rule 5), at the
same tier as callout accuracy and the polish bar already are in the Part 4
pre-send checklist — not a mechanical check, until a real signal for "topic
complete" exists to check against.
