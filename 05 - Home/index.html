Process Core

The shared process model — netlist, geometry, sizing, trim, thermal, flow field. A library, not an application; these are its own diagnostic pages, which is how we check it is telling the truth.

The physical layerbuilt

For showing a room. The loop at true scale, then stretched until it can be seen — with the drawing printing its own exaggeration. Then every dimension on it, sorted into measured, convention and art. A drawing that tells you which of its own lines are real.
FIC-101 — the ladderbuilt

One model, five depths: loop, line, assembly, section, detail. Click the valve to go further in. Everything at every depth is the same netlist and the same solved field.
The piping rig, cross-checkedbuilt

The same case the game’s rig settles — 36 gpm down 40 ft — computed from this stack instead, so the two can disagree in public. Then carried further: fittings as catalogue rows, ISA sizing, and what an equal-percentage cage really passes.
Pro Cutawaybuilt

The inside of a bore, magnified until it can be looked at. Not a depth of its own — a way of drawing one, with the fluid turned on.
The loop, specifiedbuilt

Cooling water to an exchanger. Every number on the page came out of a layer — service, assembly, sizing, cavitation, packing, thrust, and what is at stake when the valve will not open.
Assemblybuilt

The actuator standing on the valve, travel live off the milliamp signal. Checked against two published tables before it was drawn.
The flow labbuilt

Ten layers inlined on one page, graded against numbers somebody else published.
Parts, to scalebuilt

Every catalogue part drawn at its real size, in inches, from its published dimensions.

Interactive models

Fisher 657built

Calibration and setup. Bench set, spring adjustment, travel verification — narrated, with the geometry built from hand-painted masks over the IOM's Figure 6.

Lunar Process Engineer

The process-simulation game. Built on the Process Core, which it vendors rather than reaches into.

Playbuilt

Sizing, selection and diagnostics on a flow loop. Rebuild with node build.js if this is stale.
Piping reference rigbuilt

The rig the game's piping is settled against — arrangements, friction, applicability. It exists precisely to stop two levels drifting into different rigs, and everything that draws pipe takes its answers from here.

The cascade, and the bench

A PID loop you can drive, with a second loop inside it. The same faceplate runs against a simulated process or, with a COM port, against a real Fisher DVC6200 over HART — which is why there is one entry here and not two.

The cascade, as a plantbuilt

The pipe the water is actually in, the tank it comes from, the exchanger it goes to, and both loops over the top — with the cascade link drawn as what it is: a setpoint, not a valve signal. The valve is cut open in the line — the traced body with a 657 on it and the solved field running through its trim — and clicking it goes further in. Opens with no launcher.
The cascadestart it

Double-click LoopBench.cmd, then this opens it. TIC-101 holding temperature with FIC-101 holding flow inside it, and a supply sag on the fault pane: trip it and the flow loop has the valve open before the temperature moves. The one thing a single loop cannot be made to show.
The same faceplate, other waysarguments

LoopBench.cmd single — one temperature loop, no inner loop, for the before-and-after. LoopBench.cmd positioner — a valve and nothing else, as the real bench is. LoopBench.cmd COM4 — real hardware on that port.
