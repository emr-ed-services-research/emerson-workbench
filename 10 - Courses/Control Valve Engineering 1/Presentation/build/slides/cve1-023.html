Check Your Knowledge — Variability & Oversizing

A 6-inch butterfly valve was selected at line size for a service that only needs a fraction of its rated Cv. Bench data confirm its deadband and dynamic response are both within spec. In service, the loop hunts and its process-variability trend is wide — but only when demand drops well below design flow; at high flow it controls smoothly. What is the most likely cause?

  • A.  The valve spends most of its time at low openings, where an oversized valve's already-worse seal friction is exaggerated by its own large flow-change-per-travel — and a butterfly's control range narrows there faster than a globe's would
  • B.  Too much of the loop's total gain is concentrated in the controller rather than the valve
  • C.  Backlash or stiction in the actuator linkage is producing deadband
  • D.  The actuator/positioner combination has a slow dynamic time from an undersized air-chamber volume

A · Oversizing's low-opening friction penalty, worst on a butterfly
