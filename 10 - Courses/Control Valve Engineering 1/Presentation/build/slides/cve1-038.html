Check Your Knowledge — Guiding & Restricted-Capacity Trim

A body was specified one size larger than today's process needs, to leave room for future capacity growth. At today's flow the plug spends most of its travel near the closed end of the port and the loop hunts. The spec also correctly calls for cage-guided construction to keep the plug tightly aligned to the seat ring. What is the correct fix — and why doesn't the cage guiding already solve it?

  • A.  Switch to a stem-bonnet-guided body — cage guiding can't hold stable alignment at low travel
  • B.  Fit restricted-capacity trim (smaller-size plug/cage/seat-ring, adapter-mated to the body) — guiding stabilizes alignment, but doesn't change how much of the port's travel today's flow actually uses
  • C.  Fit a body-guided trim insert instead — body guiding gives more bearing area than a cage at low travel
  • D.  Add a pipeline reducer upstream of the valve — the body's flow area, not the guiding method, is the problem

B · Restricted-capacity trim
