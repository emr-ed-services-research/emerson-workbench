Three Criteria That Set the Body/Bonnet Spec

CriterionSets / driven byHow it couples to the others
Pressure ratingANSI class — 150/300/600 most common for steel & stainlessEach class's maximum-pressure ceiling falls as service temperature rises, per the material's own strength curve; not every product is available in every class
TemperatureBody-material strength/ductility limits, thermal expansion, soft-part limits — elastomers to roughly 200–350°F, PTFE to roughly 450°FMetal seals (needed above PTFE's limit) generally increase shutoff leakage; hot metal bearing sleeves in rotary valves add shaft friction that reduces the pressure-drop load the shaft can withstand at shutoff
Material selectionCorrosion, erosion, flashing, cavitation, and process pressure/temperature weighed togetherIn-valve velocity runs higher than pipe velocity, so valve material often differs from piping material; trim material follows body material, temperature range, and fluid properties; bar stock or lined bodies considered when the needed body material isn't available as carbon, alloy, or stainless steel
None of the three stands alone — temperature limits which materials are usable, and material choice constrains which pressure class is even available.
