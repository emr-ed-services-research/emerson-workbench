Check Your Knowledge — Actuator Mechanism Types & Selection

A rotary valve throttles continuously in a service where losing instrument air must drive the valve to a known safe position. The required torque comfortably fits a single-acting design, and plant air is available. Which actuator type and fail-safe configuration is the right starting choice?

  • A.  Double-acting piston actuator, no fail-safe accessory added — highest thrust and fastest stroking, so plan the rest of the loop around it
  • B.  Single-acting (spring-return) spring-and-diaphragm actuator, rotary construction — inherently fail-safe and throttles without a positioner; start here unless torque or speed rules it out
  • C.  Electric actuator with a backup battery for the fail-safe position — cheapest to install where plant air already exists
  • D.  Rack-and-pinion actuator with a positioner added for continuous throttling — compact and economical for a rotary body

B · Single-acting spring-and-diaphragm (rotary)
