Check Your Knowledge — Force & Torque Sizing

You calculate a sliding-stem valve's total required force at 300 lbf. A candidate diaphragm actuator has bench set 8–20 psig, 60 sq. in. effective area, and an operating range of 4–20 psig — giving 240 lbf of precompression, a real shortfall against the 300 lbf required. A colleague suggests fixing it by installing a bigger actuator with double the effective area. What is the more careful next check before doing that?

  • A.  None needed — a bigger actuator is always the safer fix for a force shortfall
  • B.  Confirm the larger actuator's thrust doesn't exceed the valve's own maximum-stem-load rating, since too much force can buckle the stem or damage internals
  • C.  Recompute the actuator's torque rating instead of its force rating, since torque governs every actuator selection
  • D.  Switch to a rotary valve, since rotary actuators use published A/B/C torque factors instead of a force calculation
