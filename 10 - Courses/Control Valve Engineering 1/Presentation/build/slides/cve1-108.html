When Standard Actuation Runs Out

Bench set assumes a standard spring-and-diaphragm actuator working within its normal force range on compressed air. Real services regularly break one of those two assumptions — a force or torque demand a standard diaphragm can't reach, or no instrument air at the mounting location at all. Four real design answers exist: piston vs. diaphragm construction, electric motors, and electro-hydraulic units — differing mainly by power source and force ceiling, not by a single 'go bigger' default.
