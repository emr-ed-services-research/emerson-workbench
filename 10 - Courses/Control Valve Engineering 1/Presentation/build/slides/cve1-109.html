How Fail-Safe Action Actually Works

  • 1Fail-safe action always works the same way: energy is stored while the actuator is powered, then released to drive the valve to its required position when power is lost.
  • 2A spring-and-diaphragm actuator stores that energy mechanically — the diaphragm compresses the spring as pneumatic pressure loads the casing — and needs nothing extra to fail open or fail closed by construction.
  • 3Actuator types without a return spring must add the stored-energy source deliberately: a pneumatic volume tank, or a hydraulic accumulator for an electro-hydraulic unit; an electric actuator typically has no inherent fail-safe at all and instead holds its last position on power loss.
  • 4A third option beyond simple open/closed — fail-lock — pipes a pneumatic switching valve (a Fisher 164A is the named example) as a lock valve, holding the actuator exactly where it was rather than driving it to either end.
