Answering Extreme Force — Piston vs. Diaphragm

Piston
  • Large, high-output spring — sized to overcome valve fluid forces entirely on its own, loaded like a spring-and-diaphragm via a single-acting positioner.
  • Small, assist-only spring — relies on the valve's own unbalance forces to help drive fail-safe action; behaves like a plain double-acting piston in normal operation.
Higher raw thrust at higher supply pressure — but fail-safe has to be engineered in as a separate choice, not inherent to the design.
Diaphragm
The diaphragm family's own extreme-force answer — a field-reversible multi-spring construction (left) raises output past a standard single-spring design while staying field-convertible; the same approach extends to rotary valves through the actuator lever (right), with net thrust equal to diaphragm force minus opposing spring force.
