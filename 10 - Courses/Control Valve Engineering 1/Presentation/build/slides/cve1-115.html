Check Your Knowledge — Bench Set & Extreme-Service Variants

A sliding-stem valve's actuator was sized correctly, but in the field its travel comes up short of rated stroke. You check the bench set and find the spring adjuster is set well above the nameplate's lower bench-set value. Separately, this same service is being relocated to a site with no instrument air. What is the correct combined response?

  • A.  Recompute a higher force requirement and order a bigger actuator, then keep the same spring-and-diaphragm design at the new site
  • B.  Correct the bench-set adjustment to the nameplate's lower value first, since short travel is a bench-set problem, not a sizing problem — and since there is no instrument air at the new site, specify an electric or electro-hydraulic actuator instead of assuming spring-and-diaphragm still fits
  • C.  Leave the bench set as found since real springs carry manufacturing tolerance, and add a volume tank so the spring-and-diaphragm design can stay at the new site
  • D.  Switch to a piston actuator, since it's the fastest design and would resolve both the short-travel problem and the new site's requirements

B · Fix bench set, then reselect for no air
