Checking the Propane Case for Choked Flow

Same propane case as slides 146-147.
  1. Pv = 124.3 psia, Pc = 616.3 psia → Pv/Pc ≈ 0.202
  2. FF = 0.96 − 0.28·√(Pv/Pc) ≈ 0.96 − 0.28·√0.202 ≈ 0.83 (the non-water chart gives the same reading)
  3. Weigh ΔPmax against the actual service ΔP of 25 psi
  4. ΔPmax comes out larger than 25 psi — the flow is not choked
Common failureRunning the choked-flow check and assuming it must find something. The source states this plainly: “based on the small required pressure drop, the flow will not be choked.” Knowing when the check doesn’t apply is as much the skill as running it — a small required ΔP relative to the process pressures doesn’t automatically mean trouble.
Try it yourself: using this case’s own Pv, Pc, and the FF-and-ΔPmax method just shown, confirm for yourself that ΔP = 25 psi really does fall under ΔPmax before accepting it as the value that carries into slides 146-147’s Cv equation.

After Fisher Control Valve Sourcebook — Oil & Gas, Chapter 3, “Liquid Sizing Sample Problem,” printed pp. 3-6–3-8 — Pv, Pc, P1, P2, and ΔP are the source’s own worked values, plotted to scale; FF is computed from them via the source’s own equation, not source-stated. No ΔPmax value is drawn — the source gives no FL for this valve/trim to compute one.
