Choked Compressible Flow — A Velocity Ceiling

  • 1Compressible choking is a velocity ceiling, not liquid sizing’s vaporization ceiling — past a critical pressure-drop ratio, the gas reaches sonic velocity at the vena contracta and added ΔP stops increasing mass flow
  • 2The test is a ratio, not an absolute pressure: compare x = ΔP/P1 to the product Fk·xT (Fk = k/1.4; xT is the valve’s own rated pressure-drop-ratio factor, replaced by xTP when fittings are attached at the valve’s own connections)
  • 3At the critical value, Y is fixed at exactly 0.667 and can’t go lower no matter how much further P2 is dropped
  • 4Contrast: an 8-in Design V250 ball valve, natural gas, x = 0.70 against a rated-travel FkxT of only 0.129 — choked outright, and the real xT has to be reworked at the valve’s actual opening angle three times before the calculation converges
