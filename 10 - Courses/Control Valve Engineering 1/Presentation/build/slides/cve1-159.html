You’re checking a compressible service and find x = ΔP/P1 = 0.85 against this valve’s own Fk·xTP = 0.60 — already choked. If you drop P2 further so x rises to 0.90, what happens to the expansion factor Y and to the mass flow actually passing through the valve?

  • A.  Y drops further below 0.667 (following Y = 1 − x/(3·Fk·xTP) out to x = 0.90), and mass flow increases along with it
  • B.  Y climbs back toward 1.0, since a bigger pressure drop always pulls more flow through a restriction
  • C.  Y stays locked at 0.667, but the mass flow still increases because you increased ΔP
  • D.  Y stays locked at 0.667, and the mass flow does not increase — the gas is already moving at sonic velocity at the vena contracta, so dropping P2 further only wastes pressure drop
