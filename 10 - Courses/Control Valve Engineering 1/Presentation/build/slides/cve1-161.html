!Don’t Crowd the Piping Around the Valve

A close-coupled reducer sized down to the valve, with block valves matched to valve size, cuts a typical 2-inch valve’s real capacity by roughly 5% — a loss that grows toward 10% as valve size increases at the same 2:1 ratio, and toward 15% once elbows are added between the reducers and the valve. Moving the block valves out to line size, clear of the reducers, removes most of the effect. Avoid an inlet arrangement that creates a nozzle effect, avoid close-coupled inlet block valves reduced from line size, and avoid sharp turns close to the inlet — straight-line sections into and out of the valve should reproduce the piping the valve’s own capacity and characteristic data were established against, generally 10–20 pipe diameters upstream and 5–10 diameters downstream. Get this wrong and a correctly sized valve can lose up to 15% of its rated capacity — a shortfall the installation caused, not the valve.
