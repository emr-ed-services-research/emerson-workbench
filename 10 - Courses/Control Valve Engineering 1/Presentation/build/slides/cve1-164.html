Why Velocity Limits Gas and Liquid Differently

  • 1Gas/vapor service: no velocity limitation at all except where noise is a concern — Fisher’s published ceilings are 0.3 Mach for Whisper Trim III, 0.5 Mach for Whisper Trim I
  • 2Liquid, top-and-bottom-guided: older velocity guidelines head off guide wear, vertical instability (“buffeting”), body erosion, and noise
  • 3Liquid, cage-guided: the cage shields the plug and guides it exactly where the pressure drop occurs, so it runs at full rated pressure drop without the guide-wear risk; a balanced trim minimizes the forces that cause buffeting
  • 4Counter-intuitive: in clean fluid there is NO correlation between velocity and body erosion — real erosion traces to cavitation, flashing, wet-steam erosion, or abrasive fines, not velocity
  • 5Net rule: size on capacity requirements first — velocity-driven sizing is the exception, particularly for cage-guided valves

After Fisher Power & Severe Service Sourcebook, Ch. 13, “Velocity Limitations in Control Valves,” printed pp. 13-2–13-3 — the Whisper Trim Mach ceilings, the cage-guided mechanism, and the no-correlation-with-erosion finding transcribed as stated. Component Index: pss-topic-velocity-limitations.
