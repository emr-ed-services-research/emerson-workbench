Check Your Knowledge — Piping Arrangement & Line Sizing

A 12-inch line feeds an 8-inch, cage-guided control valve in clean liquid service. The valve passes both line-size rules of thumb — well above the ½-line-size floor, and exactly at the two-sizes-below-line floor. The block valves are welded in tight against a reducer machined down to the valve’s own 8-inch size, and an elbow sits two diameters upstream of the inlet. What is the real problem with this installation?

  • A.  Sitting exactly at the Rule 2 floor means the 8-inch valve actually fails the line-size requirement
  • B.  Because the valve is cage-guided, velocity limitations are a bigger concern here than they would be on a top-and-bottom-guided valve
  • C.  The close-coupled reducer, block valves at valve size, and the elbow near the inlet will cut the valve’s real capacity regardless of the line-size and velocity checks passing
  • D.  Since the valve passes both line-size rules of thumb, the installation is compliant and will deliver its full rated capacity

C · Piping arrangement, not sizing, is the real problem
