Built Right, Damaged Before Startup

A valve that was correctly selected, sized, and correctly piped can still fail within days of startup — not from a design flaw, but from what happens to it during welding and commissioning, before it ever sees real process fluid. Welding a buttweld or socket-weld end creates unusually high temperatures right at the valve that can damage trim; the system flush that clears the slag afterward can damage trim of its own. Both events are staged around the valve, not performed by it — and both are avoidable once the staging is understood.
