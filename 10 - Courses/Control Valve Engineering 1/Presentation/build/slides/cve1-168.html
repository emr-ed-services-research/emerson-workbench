Three Stages, Three Trim Risks

  • 1Preheat — required by piping code before the weld starts, applied with heating pads or a torch; reaches roughly 300–500°F depending on material and joint thickness. Not damaging to most metal parts, but elastomeric and plastic parts (O-rings, seal rings, backup rings, packing rings, gaskets) can have lower limits. Little risk in the packing box area itself — the bonnet and body dissipate heat — but the body interior runs closer to the full preheat value.
  • 2Welding — interpass temperature — the temperature the first weld puddle has cooled to by the time the pass comes back around to it; must stay below a material-dependent maximum, typically the same 300–500°F band for chrome-moly and stainless steels, so the same elastomeric/plastic caution applies.
  • 3Post-weld heat treating (PWHT) — the most severe stage: welding chrome-moly steel creates residual stresses that can crack the weld unless relieved by holding the area at up to roughly 1400°F for several hours. That heat conducts back into the valve body and can damage elastomeric/plastic parts, warp metal parts, or loosen shrink-fit and threaded connections.
