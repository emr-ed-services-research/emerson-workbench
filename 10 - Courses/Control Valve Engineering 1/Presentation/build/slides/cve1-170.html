Flushing Trim Stands In for the Real Trim

  • 1System flushing — water or steam flowed through the piping to clear weld slag and other solid particles before startup — and chemical cleaning both put real trim at risk; a valve is a restriction in the flow stream.
  • 2Drilled-hole or small-orifice trim (Whisper Trim, Cavitrol Trim, MicroFlat, or standard drilled-hole trim) is especially exposed: solid particles can lodge in it and cause plugging, score the plug against the cage, or nick the seating surface.
  • 3Chemical cleaning carries a different risk — cleaning solutions can etch seating or gasket surfaces, especially if not neutralized afterward or left standing in the body.
  • 4Fisher’s mitigation for both: sacrificial flushing trim — the cage or cage flange, the seat ring, a double-nutted stem sealing the bonnet stem hole, and gaskets — installed in place of the real trim for the flush or the chemical clean.
  • 5Because many different acids and cleaning solutions can be used for chemical cleaning, Fisher recommends the real trim never be exposed to the cleaning solution at all.
