The Business Case That Closes the Course

  • Reduced methane loss — gas that used to vent or bleed to atmosphere stays in the line.
  • Reduced regulatory testing burden — fewer, or less frequent, mandated leak checks once the source is closed.
  • Additional saleable natural gas — gas that no longer vents is gas the operator can still sell.

In many real cases these three savings, by themselves, are enough to pay for the instrument or actuator upgrade — sizing the valve, selecting the trim, calculating the actuator’s force or torque, and choosing the packing system were never separate from this: they’re the line items that make an operator’s net-zero target something an engineer can actually deliver, not a number Corporate reports with nothing behind it.

After Control Valve Handbook, 6th ed., §11.9, pp. 246–247 — “the savings from reduced methane emissions, reduced regulatory testing requirements, and additional saleable natural gas production pay for the instrument/actuator upgrade itself,” stated directly in source prose; no figure registered.
