```yaml
id: pp-topic-black-liquor-preparation-methods
kind: topic
teaches: >
  Two competing approaches to preparing black liquor for the furnace,
  and why the industry moved from one to the other. Older/conventional
  design: direct contact evaporators (cascade or cyclone type) use the
  boiler's own induced-draft fan to pull hot flue gas into direct contact
  with the liquor, evaporating water from 45-50% to 60-65% solids —
  paired with black liquor oxidation (a blower forcing air through a
  liquor-filled tank via a sparger ring) to convert sulfide compounds to
  a more stable form before that direct flue-gas exposure, preventing
  hydrogen sulfide/mercaptan release. Modern "low odor" design eliminates
  both: falling-film/cascade evaporators (rotating hot plates/tubes,
  alternately submerged and flue-gas-exposed) plus indirectly heated
  concentrators (raising solids to 65-70%) never let flue gas touch the
  liquor directly, so oxidation is unnecessary — at the cost of needing
  additional boiler economizer section(s) to recover the flue-gas heat
  that direct contact used to absorb. Both evaporator types also carry a
  real fire risk (high flue-gas temperature plus liquor fuel), managed by
  steam injection to displace oxygen and smother a fire.
concept-tags: [direct contact evaporator, black liquor oxidation, low odor design, cascade evaporator, cyclone evaporator, falling film evaporator, sparger ring, fire prevention]
```

```yaml
id: pp-topic-liquor-preparation-and-furnace-introduction
kind: topic
teaches: >
  The final preparation stage before liquor reaches the furnace, and the
  safety logic built into it. Density (percent solids) is measured
  between the liquor heater and liquor guns via magnetic flow meters,
  because a smelt/water explosion or bed "blackout" becomes possible if
  solids drop too low: below 60% solids, auxiliary fuel is added; below
  57-58%, the liquor is diverted away from the furnace to the mix tank
  entirely, and each liquor feed line's valve is tied to emergency
  shutdown interlocks that snap closed on a trip signal. Liquor is heated
  to 230-250°F beforehand for the right viscosity and burning
  characteristics — older designs heat directly with steam, but most
  newer designs heat indirectly specifically to avoid adding water
  content to the liquor (a safety and efficiency consideration, not just
  a design preference). Flow/pressure to the furnace is commonly
  controlled by recirculating excess liquor back to the mix tank, which
  lets the nozzle pumps run at constant speed and keeps liquor moving to
  avoid plugging. Black liquor itself is never used to start up a cold
  boiler — natural gas or fuel oil brings the boiler to a prescribed
  temperature first, specifically to avoid a smelt-water explosion in the
  lower furnace before liquor firing begins.
concept-tags: [liquor divert, solids monitoring, magnetic flow meter, emergency shutdown interlock, indirect steam heating, recirculation, auxiliary fuel, smelt-water explosion]
```

```yaml
id: pp-topic-combustion-air-staging
kind: topic
teaches: >
  Why combustion air is introduced at three separate levels (primary,
  secondary, tertiary) rather than all at once, and what "balanced draft"
  means. Primary air ports sit a few feet above the hearth and are
  designed for as low a velocity as practical while still supplying
  50-65% of total combustion air — low velocity here matters because high
  velocity would disturb the smelt bed's shape. Secondary and tertiary
  ports run at higher velocity specifically to ensure complete mixing and
  combustion of the unburned gases the primary stage doesn't fully
  consume. Combustion flue gas is then pulled from the furnace to the
  boiler's convective section by induced draft fan(s), creating a
  slightly negative pressure inside the boiler — "balanced draft" — which
  prevents hot gases from escaping boiler openings.
concept-tags: [combustion air staging, primary air, secondary air, tertiary air, smelt bed shape, induced draft, balanced draft]
```

```yaml
id: pp-topic-sootblower-valve-selection-rationale
kind: topic
teaches: >
  A complete, self-contained control-valve selection case for recovery-
  boiler sootblowers. Why sootblowing exists: boiler-tube heat-transfer
  efficiency depends on keeping tubes free of combustion-deposit
  build-up, and this cleaning must happen online, using high-pressure air
  or steam. Air vs. steam tradeoff: air systems have simpler piping (no
  condensate drain lines) but are limited by compressor count/capacity;
  steam systems have a virtually unlimited supply (drawn after the
  primary superheater) but need more valves and condensate-handling
  piping — neither is categorically better. Real typical process
  conditions: P1 = 800-1400 psig, P2 = 0 psig, T = 400-800°F. Design
  drivers that follow directly from those conditions: high pressure-class
  rating; Class V tight shutoff (so valves don't leak steam and to
  protect trim, since sootblowers fire intermittently); noise/vibration/
  wear resistance (large pressure drops); cyclic-duty durability (fired
  many times a day). The real product answer: an ES cage-guided globe
  valve (unbalanced plug) for on/off, quick-opening, Class V duty, or an
  ED (balanced plug) for throttling duty at Class II — both offering
  hardened trim and reinforced stem/plug assemblies for high pressure-drop
  service, with engineered anti-noise trim (Whisper Trim I, Whisper Trim
  III, or WhisperFlo) plus an inline diffuser where full attenuation at
  the valve isn't sufficient alone.
concept-tags: [sootblower, tight shutoff, Class V, ES valve, ED valve, cage-guided globe valve, anti-noise trim, Whisper Trim, cyclic duty]
```

```yaml
id: pp-topic-black-liquor-combustion-and-chemical-reduction
kind: topic
teaches: >
  How black liquor actually burns and regenerates cooking chemicals.
  Liquor enters the furnace through nozzles or liquor guns (stationary or
  oscillating, spraying coarse droplets onto the walls or into the
  furnace center depending on manufacturer) — the control valve feeding
  these guns needs careful material selection specifically for
  corrosion resistance, since it's exposed to both hot liquor and flue
  gas. As the sprayed organics burn and release heat, the remaining char
  (the sodium and sulfur cooking chemicals) falls to the smelt bed on the
  furnace floor, which operates at 1600-2500°F. Primary-air oxygen at the
  bed reduces sulfate to sulfide there. The resulting molten smelt
  (mostly Na2CO3 and Na2S) flows by gravity to the dissolving tank, which
  is filled with weak wash specifically to cool the smelt and produce a
  pumpable solution — green liquor — which then moves on to the
  recausticizing area to regenerate white liquor. The dissolving tank
  vents to atmosphere through a tall stack; because the smelt's sodium/
  sulfur compounds are a real odor source, that vent gas is typically
  scrubbed using weak wash as the scrubbing medium.
concept-tags: [liquor guns, char bed, smelt bed, chemical reduction, dissolving tank, green liquor, scrubber, corrosion-resistant materials]
status: current
```

```yaml
id: pp-topic-ash-handling-and-salt-cake-makeup
kind: topic
teaches: >
  Two related design/chemistry decisions in ash handling downstream of
  combustion. Precipitator design: ash (mostly salt cake, Na2SO4)
  collects from flue gas at the electrostatic precipitator and mixes with
  liquor. Older "wet bottom" designs let liquor pool at the precipitator
  bottom with salt cake falling directly in — a real fire hazard and odor
  source — so most newer precipitators use "dry bottom" design instead,
  where chains or screws convey the dry ash into a separate liquor-filled
  sluice tank, avoiding that pooled-liquor risk entirely. Salt cake
  chemistry: before the recovery furnace, liquor is blended with
  additional ash/salt cake in a mix tank to replace the small chemical
  losses the recovery cycle incurs (sourced from boiler fallout hoppers
  and purchased make-up, with direct steam heating to keep the liquor
  pumpable) — but because salt cake carries high sulfur content, some
  mills have substituted sulfur-free caustic soda (NaOH) or soda ash
  (Na2CO3) instead specifically to reduce emissions.
concept-tags: [electrostatic precipitator, wet bottom, dry bottom, salt cake, sodium sulfate, caustic soda, soda ash, emissions reduction]
```
