The cascade, as a plant

Not a faceplate. The pipe the water is actually in, the tank it comes from, the exchanger it goes to, and both loops drawn over the top — the temperature controller setting the flow controller’s setpoint, which is the whole of what a cascade is.

what is on the drawing

tagwherewhat it does
FT-101 in the field flow transmitter in the line
FIC-101 control room the INNER loop -- holds flow, drives the valve
LT-103 in the field Level-Trol displacer on TK-1 — measurement only here
TT-102 in the field temperature transmitter on the product leaving
TIC-102 control room the OUTER loop -- holds temperature, sets FIC-101

The orange run is the cascade link: TIC-102’s output is not a valve signal, it is FIC-101’s setpoint. That one line is the difference between two loops near each other and a cascade.

what is real here, and what is not

  • The pipe is real. NPS 6 Schedule 40, bore 6.065 in from ASME B36.10M through a catalogue that refuses to load if the printed and canonical values disagree. 40 ft of it, declared.
  • The water speed is real. 6.2 ft/s — 560 gpm through that bore, and nothing else.
  • The bore is drawn ×8 against length, and says so under the drawing. At true scale this pipe is a wire; every schematic stretches it and this one tells you by how much.
  • The tank and the exchanger are ART. No standard we hold dimensions the shape of a vessel. They are drawn because the netlist has always had TK-1 and HX-101 in it and nothing had ever put an outline round them — phys-view draws pipe and deliberately stops.
  • Nothing here is controlling anything. The Process Core has no PID and no clock. This is the arrangement, which is a netlist fact. The cascade that actually runs is next door in LoopBench — LoopBench.cmd — and the two are meant to be shown together: this is what the plant is, that is what the loop does.
