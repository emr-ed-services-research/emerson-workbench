1 · a straight run NPS 10 Sch 40 — the profile and nothing else

Bore 10.02 in, drawn to scale. The fluid drags on the wall, so the middle runs fast and the film against the bore barely moves. Every other view in this core draws this line as a line.

2 · through a fitting NPS 1/2 socket-weld elbow — the bore changes

The socket is 0.874 in and the running bore is 0.6535 in, so the fluid must speed up by the area ratio, 1.79×, and slow down again. Nobody told the drawing that — it is continuity against the catalogue’s own dimensions. Note the square corner: through a socket-weld fitting the bore really does turn one.

3 · the valve EWT NPS 10x8 equal-percentage — the fluid, solved

A globe valve body is not a bore with a pinch in it. It is two chambers, stacked and offset, with the port as the only opening between them — which is measured, not assumed: the two chambers come back as two components that never touch. Every outline is read off Figure 11-5 of the Oil & Gas sourcebook, and that figure is near enough to scale, so the casting, the chambers and the trim all place off one published dimension.

The fluid is solved, not drawn. Incompressible, irrotational, depth-averaged: velocity is the gradient of a potential, no flow crosses a wall, and the through-flow is pinned to the real volumetric flow at the flanges. Depth out of the page is set so every cross-section equals the area the catalogue gives — the window's from the derivative of the published Cv curve, the port's from the published port diameter — so the velocity ratios come back out as the valve's own. Flow is Q = Cv√(ΔP/sg), capped where ISA-75.01.01 says it chokes.

A particle here is fluid, not flow. They are seeded by area over the whole wetted cavity at one density and carried at whatever the field says — a sprint through the window, a drift in the corners, nothing at all in the drillings through the plug or the cavity over its head, which are full of liquid and carry none of it. That is what makes the plug balanced.

Declared: this is a plane cut through a three-dimensional casting, played at 1/50 speed. Being irrotational the solve can have no eddies at all, so the swirl in the slow corners is added on top at 18% of the neighbouring stream and nothing else depends on it. The pressure minimum is not Bernoulli’s — an inviscid field is singular at a sharp corner — it is the valve’s published FL.

5 · the straight run panel 1, with a valve in the line

This is panel 1. The same flow path, the same field, the same single population of markers, the same draw call — with the valve put on the path instead of beside it. Every earlier attempt at a line with a valve in it built two systems: a solved field for the valve, a straight run for the pipe, and arithmetic in between to make them agree at the flange. That arithmetic is where every seam came from. Here plant.route composes line, valve, line into one geometry flow path and nothing below it changes — a marker crosses no boundary because there is none to cross. It speeds up through the trim by continuity alone, and the wall still drags on it either side.

4 · laminar, transition, turbulent why the profile is blended

The same line at three Reynolds numbers. Laminar is a sharp parabola with the centreline at exactly twice the mean; turbulent is blunt with the centreline at 60/49. Between them the flow is genuinely part one and part the other, so the profile blends rather than snapping — which is the honest plot as well as the readable one.
