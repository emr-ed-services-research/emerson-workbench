The piping rig, cross-checked

Lunar Process Engineer has a reference rig, and its README says it exists “precisely to settle” the piping so two levels cannot drift into different rigs. This is the same rig computed from the Process Core. Two rigs answering one question from different stacks are only worth having if somebody compares them — so that is the first thing here, and it fails loudly.

1 · the shared case

36 gpm, 40 ft of Schedule 40, 60 psig available at the manifold. Taken verbatim from reference/friction.js: Darcy-Weisbach with the Swamee-Jain explicit friction factor, commercial-steel roughness ε = 0.00015 ft. The bores come from this repository’s catalogue, which will not load unless each dimension matches what ASME B36.10M printed. Two independent transcriptions of one table.

Everything from v rightward is computed here from the Core bore. The exercise this case comes from turns on the last column: pick the pipe before you run the bay and the bay is starved.

And the honest gap: pipe friction is not in the Process Core. There is no Darcy-Weisbach anywhere in src/. This page computes it, and a lab page computing something does not put it in the stack. The Core holds the bores; the friction above is the rig’s arithmetic, not a layer’s answer. A rig that quietly implements the thing it is testing is not a test.

1b · temperature, and where laminar would be

Every friction number above is a function of viscosity, and viscosity is a function of temperature. Neither rig says what temperature its ν = 0.0000108 ft²/s belongs to. The Core is stricter than both of them: flowfield.js refuses to default a viscosity at all — “No default: water at one temperature” — and throws rather than guess.

So this does not invent a viscosity-temperature table. We hold no source for one, and a plausible table is exactly what this page exists to refuse. Here is the sensitivity instead: the same case either side of the assumed viscosity, so you can see how much the answer moves and whether anything changes regime.

Loss in psi over the 40 ft run. Doubling the viscosity moves the small bores by a few per cent and the large ones hardly at all — which is what fully rough turbulent flow does, and is worth seeing rather than being told.

Nothing here is anywhere near laminar, and that is a finding rather than a relief. The gentlest row is the 3 at Re 36,986. To bring it below Re 2300 the fluid would need ν ≈ 1.74e-4 ft²/s — 16 times thicker than the water assumed. Water does not get there; a heavy oil would. The rig applies Swamee-Jain, which describes turbulent flow in a rough pipe, and the first version of this page applied it to every row without asking. It was right — by luck. A rig cannot be right by luck about the thing it is checking, so the regime is now classified per row, laminar takes 64/Re exactly, and the band between Re 2300 and 4000 returns no answer at all rather than a guess wearing four decimal places.

2 · what this stack adds — fittings that are real parts

The game’s rig has pipe and nothing in it. A run is elbows, tees and reducers, and here each one is a catalogue row with its dimension carried as the standard printed it beside the canonical value — the loader re-derives one from the other and refuses the catalogue if they disagree. That is what makes a geometry claim checkable rather than asserted.

None of them declares a resistance. 8 fittings, no K values. The dynamics layer will use a sourced resistance when a component declares one — that machinery exists and is opt-in — but nothing we hold publishes K for these, so nothing does. The loss through a fitting is therefore not in the Core’s answer for this rig, and this page would rather say so than carry a plausible number into a cross-check whose whole purpose is catching plausible numbers.

5 · what this rig does NOT answer

Written down because the useful half of a rig is the half that says where it stops.

questionwhere it stands
pipe friction computed on this page, not in the Core. No Darcy-Weisbach in src/.
fitting resistance the layer is there and is opt-in; no fitting we hold publishes a K, so none declares one.
viscosity vs temperature no source held. Sensitivity shown above; no table invented.
gas and compressible flow not in this stack at all. The sizing layer is sizeLiquid and nothing else — no expansion factor, no critical pressure ratio. LPE’s rig has a GAS SERVICE — AIR section; this one cannot answer it and does not pretend to.
two-phase, flashing downstream the sizing layer reports the choked drop and the cavitation margin; what happens after the vena contracta is not modelled.

6 · where to look next

The game’s rig is at 50 - Lunar Process Engineer/reference/cve-reference-rig.html and carries what this one does not: the turbulent velocity profile drawn rather than tabulated, the reducer / throat / expander behaviour, the gas service, and the cavitation-versus-flashing distinction. The two are meant to be read together, which is why the first section here exists.
